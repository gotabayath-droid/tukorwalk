Halloween on Khao San Road
Bangkok's open-air costume street festival

High-energy, budget-friendly nightlife on one road: a parade of gold-painted Thai dancers, costumes from horror to Thai-flavored, a club costume show and street food on every corner. Free to watch, and about 400฿ for the whole night.

Quick answer

Halloween on Khao San Road is a free open-air street festival in Bangkok: a parade of gold-painted Thai dancers and stilt walkers, costumes from horror characters to Thai-flavored looks, a club costume show, street food and roadside stalls, all on one road. From Chatuchak Park it is an MRT ride to Sam Yot (44฿), then a walk or bike ride. A full night costs about 400฿.

Why Khao San Road for Halloween

Halloween in Bangkok happens in many places, from mall events to club parties across the city. This guide picks one: Khao San Road, for three reasons.

  • High-energy, budget-friendly nightlife. Street food, drinks, massage, souvenirs and costumes all sit on one road, and most of it costs very little.
  • A unified open-air street festival. Instead of scattered venues, the parade, the costume show, the decorated bars and the vendors share a single road, and watching is free.
  • Bangkok's ultimate Halloween destination, for a solo traveler. It is easy to reach by MRT, families and tourist police share the road, and it is easy to leave.

Getting there: MRT Chatuchak Park → Sam Yot

The route is short and cheap. Start at MRT Chatuchak Park (next to Mo Chit BTS) and ride to Sam Yot for 44฿. From the station, Khao San Road is about 20 minutes on foot or about 10 minutes by Anywheel shared bike; tuk-tuks wait in front of the station for anyone who prefers one.

The street festival: setup, decorations and crowds

The whole road is one open-air party. A giant glowing foot statue marks the main entrance, and the event schedule is posted beside it. Earlier in the evening the road is still being dressed: scaffolding wrapped in bloody fabric, bars and clubs adding cobwebs, props and red neon, and a creepy clown standing guard. Vendors sell glowing pumpkins and devil horns, and face-painting and henna stalls fill the pavement.

The crowd

By about 7:30 PM locals and foreigners pour in, and many of them are in full costume: Thai families, couples and groups of friends get into it as much as the travelers do. Halloween here is not just a party for foreigners. It is a family affair.

The parade: the main event

The parade is the heart of the night, and it has a strong Thai flavor: gold-painted Thai dancers in traditional golden headdresses pose for the crowd, masked figures in vine-wrapped costumes walk the road, stilt walkers tower over the street, white-masked performers carry glowing eye lanterns, and a glowing float rolls through. The schedule posted on the road gives exact times.

Tip: The parade and the costumes are completely free to watch. Just be on the road at the right time: no ticket, no VIP zone.

Costumes on the road

Most of the costumes on the road are not rental outfits but looks people clearly planned: horror characters, movie villains and cosplay, alongside Asian-inspired costumes that give Bangkok's Halloween its own character.

Clubs and the costume show

After dark the clubs take over. A monster-faced stage hosts the costume show and draws the biggest crowd, music pours from every bar, roadside counters serve drinks, and game stalls offer cheap fun along the pavement. Drinking is optional, and this guide does not promote any specific bar.

Food, drinks and prices

Dinner on this night was pad thai for 70฿, a genuinely big portion, and water for 20฿. The menu below shows the prices at the sit-down restaurant where the dinner was eaten.

Street food

Street carts are cheaper than restaurants: pad thai is under 100฿, other roadside dishes 40–70฿ and most vendor snacks 40–50฿. Small snacks and drinks through the whole night came to 200฿ at most.

Sit-down restaurants on the road range from 100 to 500฿, and their prices call for a second look before ordering.

Services, safety and getting out

Along the road

The road has what a night out needs: ATMs and money exchange (Thai ATM fees are typically around 220฿ per withdrawal, so withdraw larger amounts and check your bank's fee), toilets at 10฿ for both men and women, roadside massage centers (from 180฿), and small boutique-style stores and souvenir shops selling cheap gifts and clothes, with dresses from around 100฿.

Safety

Even when the road is packed, it does not feel sketchy. Families and children are out in force, and tourist police are visible everywhere, patrolling, helping people and giving security and emergency support. The Tourist Police hotline is 1155. In dense crowds, keep your bag, wallet and phone close and in front of you.

Getting out

Taxis are the expensive way out: most do not use the meter and quote about double the fair price. The better options are an Anywheel bike to Sam Yot MRT (about 10 minutes) or a walk (about 20 minutes) along calm, quiet roads. If the main road is blocked at both ends, narrow side lanes lead out, and the streets outside Khao San Road have plenty of restaurants and a much quieter night.

Halloween on Khao San Road: questions

Why choose Khao San Road for Halloween in Bangkok?

Halloween in Bangkok happens in many places, but Khao San Road combines high-energy, budget-friendly nightlife with a unified open-air street festival: the parade, costume show, decorated bars, vendors and food all share one road, and watching is free.

Is the Halloween event on Khao San Road free?

Yes. Watching the costumes and the parade costs nothing: no ticket and no VIP zone. Food, drinks, face painting (from about 200฿) and massage (from 180฿) are optional extras.

What happens at the Halloween event on Khao San Road?

A street festival on the road itself: a parade of gold-painted Thai dancers, stilt walkers and masked performers, costumes from horror characters to Thai-flavored looks, a costume show at a club stage, face-painting and henna stalls, decorated bars, street food and vendors selling devil horns and glowing pumpkins.

What time should I go?

The festival is on Halloween night, 31 October (a Saturday in 2026); a schedule is posted on the road. Around 5:15 PM the road is almost empty, which suits prices and photos. The crowd arrives around 7:30 PM, the road is packed by 8 PM and it peaks between 9 and 10 PM.

How do I get to Khao San Road by MRT?

Take the MRT to Sam Yot (44฿ from Chatuchak Park, next to Mo Chit BTS). From Sam Yot it is about 20 minutes on foot or about 10 minutes by Anywheel shared bike.

Is it safe, including for solo travelers?

The night felt like a street festival: families and children were out in force, and tourist police were visible everywhere, helping people and giving emergency support. Keep your bag, wallet and phone close and in front of you. The Tourist Police hotline is 1155.

How much does a night at the event cost?

About 400฿ at most on a budget: MRT both ways (about 44฿ each), pad thai (70฿), water, small street snacks and drinks (up to 200฿ in total) and a toilet (10฿).

What is the best way to leave Khao San Road?

Skip the taxis: most do not use the meter and quote about double. Use an Anywheel bike to Sam Yot MRT (about 10 minutes) or walk (about 20 minutes). If the main road is blocked, narrow side lanes lead out.
