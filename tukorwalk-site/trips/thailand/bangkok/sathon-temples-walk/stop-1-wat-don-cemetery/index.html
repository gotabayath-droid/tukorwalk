How the walk works

This is a budget-friendly half-day walking route that starts and ends near Saphan Taksin BTS in Sathon, Bangkok. Everything is within walking distance once the station is left behind: there are no entrance fees, and no transport costs beyond the BTS ride from Siam (40฿).

The route follows Charoen Krung Road and the lanes around it through a working neighborhood. Along the way are a local duck noodle shop, laundry stations, small Thai and Chinese shrines on the roadside, office buildings, and, set back from the main road, a large newly built Chinese temple complex and a cemetery that doubles as a neighborhood wellness park. Nothing needs booking.

Each stop has its own page with the address, what it costs, how long it takes, which facilities exist and plenty of photos. Together the stops take about 3 hours 45 minutes, with roughly 37–40 minutes of actual walking. The route ends at Surasak BTS, a 15-minute walk from the last stop.

Getting there from Siam

Take the BTS Skytrain from Siam to Saphan Taksin. It costs 40฿ and takes about 20 minutes. Exit the station and walk: every stop on this route is on foot, so no taxis are needed.

Sathon Street Life: What You'll See Walking Between the Temples

Between the temples and shrines, the route passes through everyday Sathon: roadside shops, drink and food stalls, motorbike taxi riders waiting at the main road, scooters weaving past, and pedestrian crossings where office workers and locals cross on their way. Practical tips

  • Bring a hat, sunglasses and water. Daytime sun on the temple complex is harsh.
  • Carry snacks: there is no food near the White Jade shrine.
  • Dress modestly and keep your voice low at shrines and the cemetery.
  • Recharge at the free sockets in Jarikano Coffee and the White Jade rest corridor.
  • Check out of the hotel by 11 AM.

Honest notes from the visit

  • The room Small and narrow. No soap, toothbrush or drinking water, so bring your own

  • Food None at the White Jade shrine and few options between stops. Carry snacks
  • Heat Little shade in the temple gardens by day. Start early

FAQ: Sathon walking route

How much does a half-day walk in Sathon, Bangkok cost?

About 160฿ (BTS 40฿, noodles 40฿, coffee 80฿) with 0฿ entrance fees. With the 500฿ room it totals 660฿ plus the 40฿ BTS ride.

Are the temples in Sathon free to enter?

Yes. All stops on this route were free to enter.

How do I get to Saphan Taksin BTS from Siam?

Ride the BTS for about 20 minutes. The fare is 40฿.

How long does the route take?

About 37–40 minutes of walking and 3 hr 45 min in total. Plan 4–5 hours at a relaxed pace.

How do I get back from Wat Don Cemetery?

Walk 15 minutes to Surasak BTS or take a rider for about 40฿.

Is there free drinking water and phone charging?

Yes, at the White Jade Tai Hong Gong Shrine (water and sockets) and sockets at Jarikano Coffee.

Is the Sathon walk suitable for solo female travelers?

Yes. The Sathon walk is well suited to solo female travelers. All the places on the route are safe, public, and camera-friendly, so you can explore and take photos without worry. Many visitors do this walk alone and find it relaxed and comfortable.

What should I wear for the Sathon temple walk?

There are no strict dress requirements, and you're free to wear whatever is comfortable. That said, since the route passes through temple grounds, it's good practice to dress respectfully — for example, covering your shoulders and knees. Comfortable shoes are recommended for walking. As long as you're respectful of the local culture, anything reasonable is fine.

Can I do this walk in the afternoon?

Yes. The walk can be done at any time of day — morning, afternoon, or evening all work well. Each time of day offers a different atmosphere, so pick whatever fits your schedule best.

Stop 1 of 9 · Where to stay

Jasaen Stylish Hotel
a clean, quiet 500฿ room in Sathon

A clean, quiet 500฿ budget room ten minutes' walk from Saphan Taksin BTS: a loft bed, a shared bathroom and a calm Sathon street, with a full tour of every corner.

✓ 500฿/night✓ Real prices paid✓ Walked in person, solo

Jasaen Stylish Hotel is the base for this walk: a small budget stay at Charoen Krung 57 in Yan Nawa, about 500 m (ten minutes on foot) from Saphan Taksin BTS. The cheapest room costs 500฿ a night for a loft bed with a shared bathroom; a room with its own bathroom costs 700฿.

Check-in starts at 3 PM and takes only a few minutes. Reception is helpful: staff guide new arrivals to the room and explain everything clearly. A 500฿ deposit is collected at check-in, and check-out is at 11 AM sharp. Guests are a mix of Asian and European travelers, and the neighborhood is quiet, which makes the place ideal for a short stay between days of walking.

Arriving: entrance and lobby

From the street, the entrance is a red door with a bicycle parked beside it. The same entrance looks different at night, when the street goes quiet. Inside there is a small lobby and a reception desk with a workspace.

Red door and bicycle, the entrance at night, and the lobby.

Reception, seating and stairs

Outdoor seating sits in front of the building, reception doubles as a workspace, and a staircase with a railing leads to the rooms. A lift also serves the upper floors, but its doors are locked manually, which suits walking guests without heavy luggage.

Outdoor seating, reception workspace and the staircase.

Room 209: a small, narrow loft room

A hallway leads from the stairs to the room, which on this stay was Room 209.

The room is small and narrow, which suits a short sleep rather than a long stay. The bed is a loft bed, reached by climbing a few steps. The linen is clean and fresh and the air-conditioning works well. A small workstation, a short entryway, a mini fridge with some storage space and a secure room door complete the room.

Loft bed with workstation, the entryway, and the mini fridge with storage.

The secure door, the loft bed and its steps, and the two mirrors (one fixed to the wall, one adjustable).

A small wash basin is built into the room, with the two mirrors beside it. Towels are provided, but there is no toothbrush, no soap and no drinking water, so these are worth bringing along.

The shared bathroom

The shared bathroom is clean and tidy, with a hot shower and a toilet with toilet paper; there is no bidet shower. The door locks from the inside, and the lock must be undone when leaving.

Bathroom door, toilet with checkered tiles, and the shower area with hot water.

Food, shops and the night

A small snack bar inside the hotel sells snacks and refreshments only; the hotel does not provide food. Directly across the road is a 7-Eleven for meals or anything else needed, and many local shops line the street. Around 11 PM to 12 AM everything closes completely. Midnight here is silent, nothing like the busy center of Bangkok. The main door is closed at night, and the room key card opens it. Smoking is strictly prohibited everywhere: in the room, on the balcony and in the lounge.

Check-out and verdict

At check-out the room is inspected and the 500฿ deposit is refunded without any issue. On this stay the other side of the room was unoccupied, so nobody shared it and the night was quiet. For a solo traveler on a budget who needs a clean, quiet place to sleep, it is a solid choice. It is a poor fit for anyone who needs on-site food, a private bathroom or easy luggage handling.

Breakfast on this walk is a bowl of duck noodle soup at Ying Charoen Duck Noodles, 404 Charoen Krung 57, two minutes on foot from Jasaen Stylish Hotel. Plenty of places nearby serve breakfast, but this small, cool Thai shop is a duck noodle soup specialist that is busy with local customers, which is why it was chosen.

One bowl costs 40฿. The portion is big, the taste is excellent for the price, and a single bowl fills the stomach completely, so no extra dishes are needed. A relaxed meal takes about 30 minutes.

The shop and the bowl

The shop sits on the same street as the hotel, so there is no navigation to do: walk two minutes from the hotel door and look for the busy front. Local customers fill it through breakfast, and the bowl arrives quickly.

Canal wall art, a minute away

Less than a minute's walk from the shop, a canal area is decorated with wall art. It needs no detour: it appears simply by walking on toward the next stop.

On to the first temple

From the noodle shop, Suthiwararam Temple is an 8–10 minute walk. On the way there are laundry and drying stations: clothes can be dropped off, then collected at the scheduled time after visiting the next places.

Suthiwararam Temple
a free, quiet Thai temple on Charoen Krung

A free Thai Buddhist temple on Charoen Krung with a white Buddha statue, two staircases to explore and modern highrises rising behind the old rooflines.

✓ Free✓ Real prices paid✓ Walked in person, solo

Stop3 of 9
TypeThai Buddhist temple
EntryFree
Time here~15 min

Suthiwararam Temple stands on Charoen Krung Road at number 223, an 8–10 minute walk from the noodle shop. Entry is free. The architecture is classic Thai Buddhist, with a white Buddha statue, and the temple is quiet: it was not crowded when visited.

Two staircases lead to different levels where the structures can be explored up close. About 15 minutes is enough to see everything calmly.

Courtyard, balustrades and highrises

Modern highrise buildings rise behind the temple, so the old balustrades and courtyard sit against a clear contrast between old and new.

Courtyard, a balustrade with a highrise beyond it, and the old-new contrast.

Roof spire, Buddha statue and gold carvings

Up close, the details reward a slow look: the roof spire against the sky, the white Buddha statue, and windows framed with gold carvings.

Roof spire, white Buddha statue, and windows with gold carvings.

Coffee on the same grounds

A coffee shop, Jarikano Coffee, sits on the same temple grounds at 233 Charoen Krung Rd, which makes the temple a natural place to take a break. It is the next stop.

A one-minute walk from the coffee shop, San Chao Mae Thapthim (新興宮, 天后聖母宮 阿媽) sits right on the roadside at 201 Charoen Krung Rd, so it needs no extra walking. It is a small Chinese shrine dedicated to the goddess Mazu (天后聖母).

Wat Borom Sathon (Wat Don)
a working temple, crematorium and columbarium

A traditional Thai temple that is also a local crematorium and cemetery, with columbarium niches, reached through everyday Sathon street life.

✓ Free✓ Real prices paid✓ Walked in person, solo
← Kanlayanavisut FoundationNext stop →
Stop7 of 9
TypeTemple and crematorium
EntryFree
Time hereShort stop

Leaving the foundation shrine, the walk continues along the road through everyday local activity, with many office buildings around. Along the route stands Wat Borom Sathon (Wat Don), at 551 Charoen Krung 63 Alley, a traditional temple.

It is also a local crematorium and cemetery. People bring the deceased here to be cremated, deposit the ashes and hold ceremonies, praying and making wishes. These are completely local activities, calm and quiet.

Street scenes between the stops

The stretch between the shrines and the temple shows ordinary Sathon at work: a roadside shop, a man checking his phone, and scooters passing by.

A roadside shop, a man with a phone, and a scooter rider.

Temple and columbarium

Visiting respectfully

This is a working place for families. Visitors should stay quiet, keep their distance from ceremonies and never photograph mourners. A short stop is enough; the main destination is next, under 10 minutes away.

Stop 8 of 9 · Main stop

The White Jade Tai Hong Gong Shrine
the walk's main stop: golden pillars, free water and sockets

A large, newly built Chinese temple complex with gardens, golden pillars, fortune sticks, free chilled water and clean restrooms. The main stop of the walk.

✓ Free✓ Real prices paid✓ Walked in person, solo
← Wat Borom Sathon (Wat Don)Next stop →
Stop8 of 9
TypeChinese shrine and garden
EntryFree
Time here~2 hours

The White Jade Tai Hong Gong Shrine, at 88 Charoen Rat Rd, is the main stop of the walk: a large, newly built Chinese temple complex with full facilities for visitors and fascinating landscaping and architecture. It is under 10 minutes on foot from Wat Borom Sathon.

More than two hours can easily pass here. The complex is clean, tidy and well maintained, with many staff to help and guide, and it is calm enough to rest in. Entry is free.

Before arriving

  • Bring drinks and snacks: between the earlier stops and this shrine there was no good place to eat or drink, and there is no food here.
  • In the daytime, wear a hat or cap and sunglasses. The sun is harsh against the buildings and stonework, although the area is beautiful.

The gardens and the main archway

A big garden with landscaping surrounds the buildings. There is a security office whose staff are very helpful, a big car park, and a lift from the car park for disabled or wheelchair users.

The exterior of the complex, the main archway, and a stone censer in the garden.

The main hall

Inside the main building, the hall is full of golden pillars and white structures. Many Chinese visitors come, and groups of Chinese tourists arrive, although the hall is not crowded all the time.

Golden pillars in the main hall, a white statue altar and the Mazu statues.

Fortune sticks

Beside the altar are fortune-telling sticks. A container is held and shaken slowly until one stick falls out. Its number is matched to a slip on the numbered shelf, and the card for that number is taken. It was done just for fun, without knowing the belief or story behind it, and many visitors do the same.

The fortune sticks altar, the numbered slip shelf, and the fortune slip.

The rest corridor: seats, fans and power

Outside the main hall a corridor offers chairs, fans or coolers and power plugs, safe, plug-based sockets where a camera battery or phone can be charged. Inside the hall it is fully relaxing, and the corridor is a good place to rest between photos.

The corridor and its red lanterns, a good place for architecture photos.

Free chilled water

The temple provides free cool drinking water from chillers. Anyone can take a bottle.

The water cooler and the free drinking water.

Restrooms and washrooms

Beside the garden are big, clean, tidy restrooms for men and women, plus a washroom for disabled visitors. A bath is possible for anyone who needs one.

The restroom, the bathroom, the restroom interior and a toilet stall.

Leaving

There is no place for food here. Back at the main road, a few motorbike riders wait, ready to take passengers to the next place, back to the hotel or to the BTS. The next stop, Wat Don Cemetery, is a 10-minute walk.

  • Time needed About 2 hours

  • Toilets Clean male, female and accessible restrooms
  • Food None on site; bring snacks and drinks

Wat Don Cemetery & Wellness Park
a Thai cemetery with a wellness park

A free Thai cemetery and wellness park: outdoor gym, jogging path, taekwondo center, a Chinese pavilion, an arch bridge and arched graves. The last stop.

✓ Free✓ Real prices paid✓ Walked in person, solo
← The White Jade Tai Hong Gong ShrineBack to →
Stop9 of 9
TypeCemetery and park
EntryFree
Time here~30 min

The last stop is Wat Don Cemetery, 10 minutes on foot from The White Jade Tai Hong Gong Shrine. The route crosses the main road and follows the other street. It is a Thai cemetery and also a wellness park, with a taekwondo training center and a gym.

Many local people exercise, play and jog here, and there is a main shrine area. About 30 minutes is enough, and entry is free.

Crossing the main road

The way in requires crossing the main road at a pedestrian crossing, then following the other street.

A neighborhood wellness park

The outdoor gym, the jogging path and a dragon pillar.

Shrine area, pavilion and arch bridge

The Chinese arch bridge and a mythical creature wall panel.

The cemetery

Thai cemetery graves lie here too, and some graves are arched in Chinese style. Smoking is totally prohibited. Photographs should be taken with care and respect for others. There is not much to see beyond the shrine area and the cemetery, so there is no need to walk the whole area. A small coffee shop sits at the entrance, and many Thai people pass through.

Chinese arched graves and the no-smoking sign.

Getting back

From here the walk can continue to other places, or head back. Surasak BTS is about 15 minutes on foot, or a motorbike rider from the cemetery costs about 40฿.
